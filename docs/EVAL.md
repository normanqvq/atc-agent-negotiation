- **Omniscient optimiser**: sees everything, picks the legal candidate with the lowest soft cost.

**Read this honestly.** With the current soft cost (delay + 2 x missed connections + 0.5 x overtime), every cost term grows with landing time, so the earliest legal slot is always the cheapest one: FCFS and the optimiser are the same plan, and Concord can only tie or lose on these numbers. What negotiation adds is not in this cost model (holding fuel vs slowing down, the original gate, ATC workload, a medical case). The measured win is safety: no final plan breaks a hard rule, and every unsafe proposal is stopped by code.

## Failure case

- **Run**: pool flight 16 (DL1126), row 246028 of flights.csv, Stress test: fog + one runway closed + low fuel + gate zone closed
- **Summary**: Stress test: fog + one runway closed + low fuel + gate zone closed: zero legal candidates for DL1126
- **What happened**: The rule layer blocked all 5 candidate slots (CAP-01 2, FUEL-01 2, WAKE-01 1). No agent was asked; the system reported 'no safe plan, consider diversion' and handed over to the duty manager.
- **Why**: Acceptance dropped to 1 arrival per 10 min, and every candidate slot hit a hard rule: #1 18:15: 18:10-18:20 already has 2 arrivals, fog + one closed runway limit is 1; #2 18:21: preceding EV5335 is Medium, gap 0 min < 2; #3 18:27: 18:20-18:30 already has 2 arrivals, fog + one closed runway limit is 1; ...
- **Fix**: Add later slots and diversion airports as candidates, so the desks can negotiate a longer hold or a diversion instead of giving up.
- None of the 60 runs above ended with no safe plan, so this case comes from a separate stress test **outside the 60 runs**: all 20 pool flights under `{"weather": "fog", "runway": "one_closed", "aircraft_status": "low_fuel", "terminal": "zone_closed"}` (the failure scenario of issue #4). 1 of 20 flights had zero legal candidates: pool 16 (DL1126). No LLM is called for those, the rule layer stops them first.

## Limitations

- **Simulated, not real data**: gate occupancy, fuel, flight and ramp crew times, connecting passengers and wake categories. They are set by the condition set (see `src/scenario.py`). Flights, delays, causes and the arrival queue are real.
- **One flight at a time.** The other arrivals are fixed at their scheduled times and never move.
- **Rules are simplified** (from `data/rules.json`): wake separation uses time gaps (3 min behind Heavy, 2 behind Medium) instead of NM; capacity scales a fixed per-runway rate by a weather factor; fuel is minutes of endurance (hold burns 1.0/min, slow-down 0.4/min); gates use a 45 min turnaround with a 10 min buffer.
- **Soft-cost weights are assumptions** (`src/rules.py`). The connection cutoff is ETA+15 min (simulated tight bank).
- **LLM answers vary between runs.** Re-running gives slightly different consensus numbers; the rule layer is deterministic.
- The arrival queue only holds up to 8 real arrivals around the flight, so real ATL traffic is denser than this.
- Not measured: fuel burned and time-to-decide in operations. The `estimated` rows in `data/sample_eval.json` are estimates.
